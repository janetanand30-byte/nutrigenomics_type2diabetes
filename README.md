# Precision Nutrigenomics of Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus in South Asians

**Integrative in silico analysis of gene–diet interactions, GWAS, eQTL, and epigenomic evidence**

## Overview

Type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) is a complex metabolic disorder influenced by genetic susceptibility and environmental factors, including diet. This project integrates genetic association data, gene–diet interaction studies, pathway enrichment, protein–protein interaction networks, expression quantitative trait loci (eQTLs), and epigenomic evidence to explore precision-nutrition research in South Asian populations.

The study uses a computational bioinformatics approach and publicly available databases to investigate how genetic variants and dietary factors may contribute to T2DM susceptibility.

## Objectives

- Identify T2DM susceptibility genes associated with dietary interactions.
- Annotate genetic variants and population-specific allele frequencies.
- Analyse genome-wide association study (GWAS) evidence.
- Investigate gene–diet interactions reported in published studies.
- Perform pathway enrichment and protein–protein interaction analysis.
- Integrate eQTL and DNA-methylation evidence.
- Explore evidence-informed, genotype-guided precision-nutrition strategies for South Asian populations.

## Candidate Genes

The analysis focuses on seven T2DM susceptibility genes:

| Gene | Biological relevance |
|---|---|
| TCF7L2 | Wnt signalling and pancreatic beta-cell function |
| FTO | Energy balance, appetite regulation and obesity |
| PPARG | Adipogenesis, lipid metabolism and insulin sensitivity |
| KCNJ11 | Pancreatic beta-cell potassium channels and insulin secretion |
| MTNR1B | Melatonin signalling and meal timing |
| ADIPOQ | Adiponectin production and insulin sensitivity |
| IRS1 | Insulin-receptor signalling and glucose metabolism |

## Methodology

The project follows an integrated bioinformatics workflow:

1. **Literature review:** Search and screen published T2DM nutrigenomics studies using a PRISMA-guided approach.
2. **Gene selection:** Shortlist susceptibility genes with reported gene–diet interactions.
3. **Variant annotation:** Retrieve SNP identifiers, genomic positions, alleles and population frequencies.
4. **GWAS analysis:** Extract association statistics, including odds ratios, confidence intervals and p-values.
5. **Gene–diet interaction analysis:** Review reported interactions involving dietary fat, dietary patterns, carbohydrates, omega-3 fatty acids and meal timing.
6. **Pathway enrichment:** Investigate biological pathways associated with the selected genes.
7. **PPI network analysis:** Examine protein interactions and network characteristics.
8. **eQTL analysis:** Investigate relationships between genetic variants and gene expression across relevant tissues.
9. **Epigenomic integration:** Review DNA-methylation evidence from publicly available GEO datasets.
10. **Interpretation:** Integrate findings to identify potential directions for precision-nutrition research.

## Key Findings

The project report describes the following findings:

- **93 eligible studies** were included in the literature synthesis.
- Seven candidate genes were selected for integrated analysis.
- Reported gene–diet interactions involved saturated fat, Mediterranean dietary patterns, polyunsaturated fatty acids, glycemic index, meal timing and omega-3 fatty acids.
- Pathway enrichment highlighted insulin signalling, PPAR signalling and T2DM-related pathways.
- PPI analysis identified IRS1 and PPARG as notable network hubs.
- eQTL and DNA-methylation evidence provided additional functional context for selected genetic variants and dietary interactions.

These findings represent the evidence synthesised in the project report and require validation against the original studies and databases before clinical application.

## Limitations

This project is based on secondary data and computational evidence rather than primary clinical experiments. Results may be affected by differences in study design, sample size, ancestry representation, dietary assessment methods and database versions.

Associations between genetic variants and dietary exposures do not independently establish causation or demonstrate that genotype-guided dietary interventions improve clinical outcomes. Further validation, particularly in South Asian populations, is needed.

## Applications

This research provides a framework for exploring:

- Nutrigenomics and personalised nutrition.
- Genetic susceptibility to complex metabolic diseases.
- Integrative analysis of GWAS, eQTL and epigenomic data.
- Functional pathway and protein interaction analysis.
- Future precision-nutrition research in South Asian populations.
